Anywhere in the run

A guide to the characters, altars and items that follow you through a run, for players of Shell Game

Anywhere in the run

Most of Spelunky 2 belongs to one area, but a few things follow you through the whole run. Sparrow and Beg are characters who show up when you've done something to earn their attention: Sparrow when you rob a shop, Beg when you wreck one of Kali's altars. Kali's altars themselves turn up on ordinary levels all through the run, and what you do at them adds up from one altar to the next.

The second half of the page is a reference to the items: what each one does, and where the base game normally hands it out.

Sparrow's quest

Sparrow is a thief, and she notices other thieves. Rob a shop early in the run and she seeks you out, then pulls you into a plan to rob Madame Tusk that runs from the Tide Pool to Neo Babylon.

It starts with a robbery in the Dwelling, the Jungle or Volcana. Pick something up in a shop and leave with it without paying. The shopkeeper comes after you with a shotgun, so be ready to run or fight, and every shopkeeper stays angry with you for the rest of the run. Then carry on: Sparrow finds you on one of the levels that follow, before you reach Olmec's Lair. Go up to her and let her talk, and she leaves you a gift for your nerve, a pile of ropes.

After that first meeting she asks you to meet her in the Tide Pool, where Madame Tusk keeps her Idol in a locked room by her Dice House. Stealing it is the Idol Heist. Carry Madame Tusk's Idol out through a level exit and the plan moves on: Sparrow waits for you in Neo Babylon, at Tusk's Vault. The Tide Pool and Neo Babylon pages walk through the heist and the vault.

In Shell Game It's the Idol itself that moves Sparrow's plan on, wherever you find it, as long as you carry it out through a level exit. Shell Game places it no earlier than the Tide Pool and no later than Neo Babylon, and that includes Madame Tusk's Palace and Tusk's Vault on 6-3. Those come after Sparrow's meeting on 6-1, so if the Idol turns up there, her quest may not reach the vault.

Beg

Beg is a wandering devotee of chaos who admires anyone willing to defy Kali. He only appears after you've destroyed one of Kali's altars, which a bomb or any other explosion will do, and he turns up on a later level. Kali punishes anyone who wrecks her altar, so be ready for it, and take anything you want from that altar before you blow it up.

On his first visit Beg comes to congratulate you, and gives you a bag of bombs to keep the chaos going. Go up to him, let him talk, and pick up his present.

Once you've met him, destroy another altar and he comes back with a bigger present: the True Crown. The second visit only comes with the first behind you, so the order is altar, Beg, altar, Beg.

In Shell Game With the True Crown Restoration option on, once Beg's second visit is over Kali's curse is lifted, if she has cursed you, and you're restored to 4 health, whatever Beg gives you.

Kali's altar

Kali is the goddess whose altars you'll find on ordinary levels in most areas: a stone slab beneath a statue of her. Lay a creature on the slab and Kali takes it, and you earn her favor. Favor builds up over the whole run: any altar counts, your favor carries from one altar to the next, and Kali rewards you when it's high enough.

Any body will do as an offering: an enemy, a cave dweller, anything that lived. Stun it rather than kill it if you can, because Kali values a living sacrifice above a dead one. Carry or throw it onto the slab and wait for her to take it, then fetch the next one. The first time your favor is high enough, she drops a gift at her altar, one of a handful of items.

Keep offering sacrifices after that first gift, the same way as before, and when your favor is high enough again she gives you a second one: the Kapala.

Kali takes Presents as well as sacrifices. Set one down on her slab and she turns it into an Eggplant. In the base game the Present comes from Great Humphead in the Tide Pool.

In Shell Game Shell Game wraps up a Present for Kali. The first time you reach a level that has both a Kali altar and a pet (a dog, a cat or a hamster), the pet becomes a Present; if the pet was for sale in a shop, the Present is too, at the same price. Pick it up (or buy it), carry it to the altar and set it down on the slab, and Kali leaves an item from your run in its place. Don't open the Present or break it anywhere else: you'll get what's inside, not Kali's item. You can't take it out of its level either, but if it's lost, Shell Game wraps up a new one on the next level that has an altar and a pet. Giving her the Present doesn't use up her first gift for favor.

In Shell Game Shell Game also changes what her altar in the Duat does. Those changes are options, described under Options on the Shell Game page.

Udjat Eye

An eye you keep once you've picked it up. It blinks when you're near a hidden entrance in the second area, and it's the key to the two secrets there. In the Jungle it blinks as you near the rock that hides the Black Market; break through to the doorway. In Volcana, carry it to the drill at the top of the level to start the dig down to Vlad's Castle, and to Vlad above it.

The base game keeps it in the gold-key chest on 1-2 or 1-3: the Udjat Eye chest.

In Shell Game Always available before the Black Market and Vlad's Castle.

Skeleton Key

A key that opens locked doors and isn't used up, so you can carry it from one lock to the next. It takes up your hands like any key: throw it ahead of you when you need to climb or fight. It opens the locked room beside Madame Tusk's Dice House in the Tide Pool, for the Idol Heist.

The base game has nowhere fixed for it: it's a rare find in shops and crates, and now and then in a pile of bones.

In Shell Game Always available before the locked room at Madame Tusk's Dice House.

Excalibur

A sword you swing in place of your whip. One swing kills most things. It's what you need against Kingu in Abzu: nothing hurts her until her shell cracks, and one swing of Excalibur cracks it. Before you set off Abzu's trap on 4-3, leave the sword somewhere safe higher up the level, because whatever you're holding when you die there is lost.

In the base game it's in the stone above the Great Lake on 4-2, which only gives it up to someone wearing the Crown or the Hedjet: Excalibur's stone.

In Shell Game Always available before the Kingu fight.

Scepter

Anubis's staff, carried in your hands. With the Crown or the Hedjet on your head, it opens the golden door on 4-2 in the Temple to the City of Gold, and through it the Duat, where Anubis II and Osiris are. In the base game Anubis drops it on 4-1.

In Shell Game Always available before the City of Gold.

Ankh

Carry it and the next time you die, you come back to life. It's used up when it does. That makes it the way into two secret levels that can only be reached by dying. On 4-3 in the Tide Pool, the trap that drains the lava from Abzu's door kills whoever sets it off, and the Ankh brings you back to go through it to Kingu. In the Temple, being sacrificed with the Ankh on the bull altar in the City of Gold takes you to the Duat, where Osiris is.

Don't spend it by accident. It goes on any death, so a fall onto spikes on the way to 4-3 or the City of Gold uses it up. It won't save a Sun Challenge either: dying in that arena fails it, Ankh or not.

The base game hides it at the top of the back layer behind Olmec's Lair, reached by riding Olmec's head down into the lava: Olmec's Ankh.

In Shell Game Always available before the Kingu and Osiris boss fights.

Crown

A crown you wear. It marks you as royalty: the stone in the Tide Pool gives Excalibur up to you, the golden door in the Temple opens for you with the Scepter, and shopkeepers give you a discount. That makes it the way to Excalibur's stone on the way to Kingu, and through the door to the City of Gold on the way to Osiris. The Hedjet does the same job at the stone and the door, so you need one or the other, not both.

In the base game it sits on the statue at the top of Vlad's Castle in Volcana.

In Shell Game Always available before the Kingu and Osiris boss fights.

Hedjet

The white crown of the pharaohs, worn on your head. It counts as a crown at the stone in the Tide Pool and at the golden door in the Temple, exactly as the Crown does, so it opens Excalibur's stone and the door to the City of Gold if you don't have the Crown. In the base game it's the priciest thing for sale in the Jungle's Black Market.

In Shell Game Always available before the Kingu and Osiris boss fights.

Alien Compass

A compass you keep once you've picked it up. In the Ice Caves its arrow points to the hidden entrance of the Mothership, with Lahamu inside it. The entrance is a single-tile ledge over the Abyss at the bottom of 5-1, in a different place every run. Line yourself up with the arrow before you drop, because missing the ledge means falling into the Abyss.

In the base game Van Horsing hands it over in the Temple, if you freed him in Volcana and he was there when Vlad died: Van Horsing's reward.

In Shell Game Always available before the Mothership and the Lahamu fight.

Eggplant

A purple eggplant you carry in your hands. It breaks easily, so set it down rather than throw it, and keep it out of fights; a hired hand can carry it for you. It's for the Eggplant Child: in the Ice Caves, set it on the altar beside the stone Moai head, and the Eggplant Child inside joins you. Keep the child alive to 7-1 and it opens the door to Eggplant World.

In the base game Kali turns a Present into one when you lay it on her altar, and the Present comes from Great Humphead in the Tide Pool.

In Shell Game Always available by the Ice Caves. Wherever it turns up, the Eggplant arrives wrapped in a Present, so it can't break on the way to you: open the Present and the Eggplant is inside. This isn't the Present for Kali, which turns up in place of a pet on a level with an altar (see Kali's altar). A Present from anywhere else holds the Eggplant, so open it rather than taking it to Kali.

Tablet of Destiny

A stone tablet you keep once you've picked it up. In Neo Babylon it tells you which of the many Ushabti statues on 6-2 is the right one. Carry that Ushabti from 6-2 to 6-3 and it hatches into the Qilin, a flying mount (see Neo Babylon). In Tiamat's Throne, once she's beaten, ride the Qilin up past the lasers to the Sunken City.

In the base game it's dropped by whichever boss you beat at the end of the fourth area: Kingu in Abzu or Osiris in the Duat.

In Shell Game Always available by Neo Babylon, and counted as part of every winning route.

Madame Tusk's Idol

A heavy golden idol that fills your hands. On its own it's treasure; to Sparrow it's proof you robbed Madame Tusk. Carry it out through a level exit and Sparrow's quest moves on toward its end at Tusk's Vault. Keep it away from Madame Tusk and her bodyguard: if they see you with it, they attack.

In the base game it's in the locked room by Madame Tusk's Dice House in the Tide Pool: the Idol Heist.

In Shell Game Available by Neo Babylon and never before the Tide Pool. It can be as late as Madame Tusk's Palace or Tusk's Vault on 6-3, after Sparrow's meeting on 6-1; Sparrow's quest says what that means for her.

Hou Yi's Bow

A bow you carry in your hands. Walk over an arrow while holding it and the bow picks the arrow up and loads it; attack to shoot it straight ahead, the way you're facing. With the Arrow of Light it's the way into the Cosmic Ocean at Hundun:

  1. Carry the bow and the arrow into 7-4, and load the arrow before the fight: drop it, then walk over it with the bow in your hands.
  2. Kill both of Hundun's heads. When it falls and opens its one big eye, it can't hurt you any more, so there's no hurry.
  3. Stand level with the eye and face it, then shoot. Ignore the door that opens at the top of the level: that's the way to the ordinary ending.
  4. If you miss, the arrow drops where it stopped. Pick it up with the bow again and take another shot, and don't let it fall anywhere you can't follow.

In the base game it's the prize of the Moon Challenge, in the Jungle or Volcana.

In Shell Game Always available before Hundun. The bow comes on its own, without the Metal Arrow the base game puts with it.

Arrow of Light

A single glowing arrow. Shot from Hou Yi's Bow into Hundun's open eye at the end of the Sunken City, it opens a portal to the Cosmic Ocean; how to load it and shoot it is under the bow. There's only one, so look after it: lose it and the Cosmic Ocean stays shut for the run.

In the base game it's the prize of the Sun Challenge in the Sunken City.

In Shell Game Always available before Hundun.

Capes and packs

The rest of the items are gear: no route needs them, but all of them help. You wear one back item at a time, and picking up another swaps it for the one you have. Unless it says otherwise, each one turns up in shops and crates.

In Shell Game The Queen Bee, whom some runs never meet, only ever gives gear like this.

Cape

Hold jump while you're falling and the cape spreads, so you drift down slowly and land without hurting yourself. It's for long drops, and for the Stars Challenge in the Tide Pool or the Temple.

In Shell Game A Cape, Vlad's Cape or the Jetpack is always placed before the Stars Challenge.

Vlad's Cape

Everything the Cape does, plus a second jump in mid-air: press jump again at the top of a jump to go higher. Good for getting up to places you couldn't otherwise reach, and for the Stars Challenge in the Tide Pool or the Temple. Vlad drops it at the top of his castle.

Jetpack

Hold jump in the air and it lifts you. It runs out of fuel after a few seconds and refills when you land, so fly in short hops from one footing to the next. Good for going straight up, crossing gaps, and the Stars Challenge in the Tide Pool or the Temple. Anubis II drops one in the Duat, and shops and crates sell them too.

Telepack

A Teleporter you wear. Jump, then press jump again in the air while holding a direction, and you jump a few tiles that way in an instant. Look before you teleport. Landing inside rock kills you; landing inside an enemy kills the enemy instead, which is how a Telepack finishes Lahamu or Tiamat in one go. Stand right under the boss and teleport up into it.

Powerpack

Powers up your attacks: your whip sets things on fire, and your bombs blow up bigger. It's a fighting item: a bigger blast clears more rock, and reaches a boss from further away.

Hoverpack

Lets you hang in the air. It can't lift you, but it can keep you where you are for as long as you like.

  1. Jump from solid ground, and press jump again at the top of the jump. You stop falling and float.
  2. While you float, move left or right to drift across. You keep the height you started at, so jump from as high as you can.
  3. Press jump again to stop hovering and drop. You can start and stop on the way down to come down in steps.

It's at its best over water, lava and spikes, and across wide gaps.

Crowns and health

Some of these you keep once you've picked them up; the health items are used on the spot.

Kapala

A cup made from a skull, which catches the blood of what you kill. Each time it fills, it empties into you and gives you back 1 health. It keeps you alive through a long run: fight the small enemies rather than avoid them, and it keeps topping you up. It's Kali's second gift at her altar.

Elixir

A potion that heals you as you pick it up, and cures poison and Kali's curse, if your dealings with her altars have earned you one. It's the prize of the Temple's Stars Challenge.

Eggplant Crown

A crown of eggplant leaves. It swaps your whip for an endless supply of eggplants to throw: fun, mostly, and a ranged attack that never runs out. It isn't the Eggplant that opens Eggplant World. The Eggplant King drops it at the top of Eggplant World: the Eggplant Crown.

True Crown

Every 22 seconds it teleports you a short way in the direction you're facing, as a Teleporter would, and gives you a stack of bombs each time. You don't choose when it goes off, so count. As the 22 seconds come round, face open space, never a wall: landing inside rock kills you. Landing inside an enemy kills the enemy. Beg gives it to you on his second visit, after you've broken two of Kali's altars.

Royal Jelly

Restores 6 health as you pick it up: a big heal before a hard fight. If you're already healthy, consider leaving it where it is until you need it, as long as you're coming back that way. The Queen Bee drops it, and there's one in Madame Tusk's Palace.

Shoes, gloves and other gear

None of these take up a hand or your back. Once picked up, they work for the rest of the run. Unless it says otherwise, each one turns up in shops and crates.

Spike Shoes

Jumping on things does far more damage, and hurts bosses that a plain jump can't, such as Quillback, the Queen Bee and Anubis. The Yeti Queen drops a pair, and shops and crates have them too.

Spring Shoes

You jump much higher.

Climbing Gloves

Jump at a wall and you cling to it; you can climb up or down it and jump off again.

Pitcher's Mitt

What you throw flies straight and fast instead of arcing. With empty hands you can catch things thrown at you.

Paste

Your bombs stick to the first thing they touch, wall, floor or monster, and stay there until they go off. A stuck bomb can't roll away, which makes short work of the Queen Bee, Anubis and Osiris, and keeps bombs on Kingu's back.

Spectacles

Lets you see the gold and gems buried in the rock, so you know where it's worth digging.

Compass

An arrow at the edge of the screen points to the level's exit. It isn't the Alien Compass, and won't find the Mothership. The Yeti King drops one, and shops and crates have them too.

Tools and weapons

Each of these fills your hands, so you can't carry another item at the same time; set it down to pick something else up. Unless it says otherwise, each one turns up in shops and crates.

Mattock

Swing it to dig through rock and earth. It breaks after a limited number of swings. It gets you into places without bombs, like the doorway to the Black Market. Tun also lends you one for the Moon Challenge in the Jungle or Volcana.

In Shell Game Tun's Mattock for the Moon Challenge isn't shuffled: she still lends you one.

Teleporter

Use it and you jump a few tiles in an instant, the way you're facing, or straight up if you hold up.

  • Look before you use it. Landing inside rock kills you.
  • Landing inside an enemy kills it outright. Stand right under Lahamu or Tiamat, keep as close to the ground as you can, and teleport up into it. If it misses, try again.
  • It can carry you across a force field without breaking it, which is how you get into Tusk's Vault without angering Madame Tusk.

Clone Gun

A gun with three shots. Whatever you shoot with it, item or creature, gets a copy beside it. It's gone once the third shot is used, so save it for something worth having twice: a bag of bombs and ropes, or a Royal Jelly. It's the prize of the Tide Pool's Stars Challenge.

Freeze Ray

Shoots a beam that freezes what it hits in a block of ice. A frozen enemy can't move or fight back, and can still be hurt, which helps against bosses: frozen, Vlad can't teleport away, and Tiamat goes down to two blows to her frozen head.

Shotgun

Fires a spray of pellets that does heavy damage up close, good against bosses with a lot of health, like Great Humphead and Tiamat. Each shot knocks you back, so don't fire it standing at the edge of a drop. Shops sell it, and every shopkeeper carries one: kill a shopkeeper and theirs is yours, at the cost of every shopkeeper's anger for the rest of the run.

Plasma Cannon

Fires a ball of plasma that explodes on what it hits, tearing through rock and monsters alike. Its shots bounce off force fields and off Olmec straight back at you, so break a force field before you fire through it, and leave it alone against Olmec. It's behind the force fields in the Mothership.

Bombs and ropes

Picking one of these up adds its bombs or ropes to what you're carrying, and that's the end of it.

Bomb Box

A crate of 12 bombs, four times what a Bomb Bag holds. It's the gift for saving all of the Three Sisters, and shops sell them too.

Bag of ropes

A bag of 24 ropes: Sparrow's reward for splitting Tusk's Vault with her.

Bag of bombs and ropes

A stitched bag of 24 bombs and 24 ropes, which comes with the prize for winning the Sun Challenge (see Sun Challenge supplies).
