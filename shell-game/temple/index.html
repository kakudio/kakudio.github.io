Temple

A guide to the Temple of Anubis, the City of Gold and the Duat, for players of Shell Game

The Temple of Anubis

The Temple of Anubis is levels 4-1 to 4-4: sandstone halls full of mummies, crush traps and crocodile men. It is one of the two ways on from Olmec's Lair; the other is the Tide Pool. You take the Temple through the door at the far right of Olmec's bottom floor, the one marked by locusts, and a run that does never sees the Tide Pool.

Two things stand on their own. Van Horsing may be waiting in 4-1's back layer, if you met him in Volcana, and Tun's Stars Challenge is on 4-2. The rest of the Temple is one road. Anubis sleeps on his throne on 4-1, and the Scepter he carries, with a crown, opens a golden door on 4-2 into the City of Gold, a hidden level that takes the place of 4-3. Dying there with the Ankh takes you to the Duat, which takes the place of 4-4 and is where Anubis II and Osiris wait. Whichever way you go, the area ends at the Ice Caves.

Van Horsing

The vampire hunter you freed in Volcana turns up again in the Temple, cursed and sure he hasn't long left. He hands you what he's carrying, the Alien Compass, which finds the Mothership in the Ice Caves.

His story starts in Volcana, so a run that went through the Jungle never sees him here. He only comes to the Temple if you:

  1. freed him from his cell on 2-1 (Van Horsing's rescue), and
  2. killed Vlad with Van Horsing there at the top of the castle, whichever of you landed the blow.

If you did both, he's waiting in a room in the back layer of 4-1. Find the door into the back layer and walk up to him.

Stars Challenge

Another of Tun's paid challenges: a dark room full of unlit torches, and a short time to light every one. The Temple's version pays out an Elixir. Tun stands outside the challenge's door on 4-2. Talk to her and pay the entry fee, $25,000 in the base game, and the way in opens.

The Tide Pool has its own Stars Challenge, but a run only ever passes through one of the two areas.

  1. Take the torch, and wear a cape or a jetpack if you have one.
  2. Light every torch in the room before time runs out. Work around the outside of the room first, then the middle, so you don't have to double back.
  3. Keep hold of your torch. If you throw it, you lose time going to pick it up again.
  4. Once every torch is lit, collect the prize.

If Tun is angry with you or dead, the challenge is closed for the run.

In Shell Game You always get a way to fly or glide before this challenge: one of the Cape, Vlad's Cape or the Jetpack is always placed earlier in the run. Bring it. The Temple's and the Tide Pool's Stars Challenges are separate checks.

Anubis and the Scepter

The jackal-headed god of the Temple sits asleep on his throne on the main path through every 4-1. When he dies he drops his Scepter, the key, with a crown, to the City of Gold.

He's far easier asleep than awake. Getting close to him, setting off an explosion nearby or hitting him wakes him: he blinks three times, then rises. Even asleep, touching him hurts.

  • Before he wakes. With Spike Shoes, jump on him while he sleeps. With Paste on your bombs, two stuck to him finish him, awake or not. Either way his 20 health goes quickly.
  • Once he's awake. He floats about, keeps out of reach of your whip, and fires slow psychic blasts from his Scepter that pass through walls and take 10 health, enough to kill you outright. After every three, an eye flashes where you're standing and a faster, bigger blast follows it there: move off that spot. Fight him with bombs and anything you can throw or shoot, from a distance, and keep moving.

When he dies, pick up his drop where he fell.

In Shell Game Anubis drops an item from your run, so the Scepter may come from somewhere else. It's never in the Tide Pool, and it's always somewhere you can get it before the City of Gold.

The City of Gold

A hidden level built entirely of gold, which takes the place of 4-3. It is the only way into the Duat, and so to Anubis II and Osiris. Expect golden crush traps that blend into the walls, mummies, crocodile men and leprechauns, and more gold than you can carry.

On 4-2 there's a door of solid gold. It opens only for a player with the Scepter who also has the Crown or the Hedjet. Watch for the crush traps near the door.

  1. Wear the Crown or the Hedjet, carry the Scepter to the golden door, and use it to open the door. Go through.
  2. Make your way to the altar near the middle of the level. It's one of Kali's altars, but with a golden bull in place of her statue, an Ankh on its forehead.
  3. If you're going on to the Duat, carry the Ankh and get yourself sacrificed on that altar: let yourself be knocked out while you lie across it, for instance by dropping onto it from a height. Kali takes you, the Ankh is used up, and you wake in the Duat.

Without the Ankh, dying on the altar is just dying. Leave by the level's ordinary exit instead and 4-4 is an ordinary Temple level.

In Shell Game The Scepter, and one of the Crown or the Hedjet, are always somewhere you can get them before the golden door. Not everything goes with you when you're sacrificed, but with the Kali Item Recovery option on, what you're holding and wearing, and durable gear you've set down by the altar (a sword, a gun, the Scepter, the Eggplant and the like), waits for you on the Duat's altar instead of being lost. See the options.

The Duat, Anubis II and Osiris

The land of the dead, which takes the place of 4-4, and the only way in is to be sacrificed with the Ankh on the bull altar in the City of Gold. Unlike every other level, you start at the bottom and climb to the top, where Osiris and his son Anubis II wait to judge you.

  1. You arrive with 4 health and without your back item.
  2. Climb. The level has no side walls, so ropes may find nothing to catch on. Snakes, cobras and Ammit roam it, and the black smoke at either edge stuns you and throws you back.
  3. Apep, a giant serpent, passes through twice on the way up. Touching its body hurts and stuns you; its head kills you outright. Get out of its path.
  4. Kali's altar is near the top, just under Apep's second pass. You can't sacrifice anything on it, but it rewards the favor you've already built with Kali.
  5. At the top, Osiris appears, and Anubis II with him.

In Shell Game The Ankh is always somewhere you can get it before Anubis II and Osiris. With Kali Item Recovery on, the items you brought are waiting on Kali's altar here. With the Balanced Duat Kali Rewards option on, the altar's rewards are bags of bombs and ropes, and Royal Jelly at the top, to win back the health you lost getting here.

Anubis II

Osiris's son joins the fight at the top of the Duat, shortly after his father appears, and always drops a Jetpack. Kill him before you take on Osiris: he summons coffins full of enemies, which are more of a threat than Osiris's fists. A single bomb kills him, and Paste makes it stick, so it can't bounce away. Pick up his drop where he fell.

Osiris

The judge of the dead: a huge floating head with two great fists. When he dies he drops the Tablet of Destiny.

  1. Deal with Anubis II first.
  2. Osiris drifts back and forth and brings a fist down whenever you pass underneath it. A slam costs 2 health and breaks the floor; running into the side of a fist stuns you and knocks you back. Touching him costs no health, but it knocks you off balance.
  3. Only his face takes damage, and he has 40 health. Bait a fist into slamming, jump onto it, and either whip his face as he drifts past or throw bombs at it. About four bombs with Paste on them does it; bombs that stick to his fists don't reach his face. Jumping on his head does nothing unless you're wearing Spike Shoes, which make it hurt.
  4. He bursts into emeralds when he dies. Grab his drop quickly: depending on where he dies, it can fall into the levels below, where Apep, enemies and lava are waiting.
  5. A portal opens where he was. It leads to the Ice Caves.

Keep away from the black smoke at either side of the arena: it stuns you and can throw you down into Apep's part of the level.
